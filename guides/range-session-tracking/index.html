Tracking range sessions by firearm

Keep a personal performance history linked to the gun you used, rather than a disconnected list of scores.

Record the session consistently

Log the date, activity, location, and the firearm used. Add your score and round count where applicable, along with notes that give the session context. Consistent entries make the history more useful than occasional scores recorded from memory.

Compare like with like

Keep Trap, Skeet, Sporting Clays, Rifle, and Pistol target practice results distinguishable. A score from one activity is not directly comparable with a different discipline or scoring system. Review changes within the same activity before drawing conclusions about a trend.

Review trends for each gun

Armur associates sessions with a specific firearm and shows performance over time. Use the history to see how your recorded results change across sessions. Notes can help explain differences that a single score cannot capture.

Keep activity and maintenance together

A firearm’s use history and maintenance records are useful companions. Record cleaning, repairs, and service separately from the session score, while keeping both attached to the same collection item. Collection exports can include the range sessions alongside the rest of its history.
