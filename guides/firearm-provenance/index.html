Documenting firearm provenance

Preserve the sources behind a firearm’s history, alongside the photographs and records that support them.

Preserve the source, not just the story

Attach invoices, correspondence, auction descriptions, and other supporting documents to the relevant firearm. Record where each document came from and the date you obtained it. Keep originals when possible; a scan is a useful working copy.

Separate evidence from interpretation

A previous owner’s account, a dated invoice, and your own research are different kinds of information. Label them clearly. When sources disagree, preserve both accounts and explain the uncertainty rather than presenting one as established fact.

Connect research to the record

Armur supports research by serial number, gun maker, or proof marks. Use the results as leads to investigate and save the supporting sources with your notes. A research match alone should not be treated as proof of ownership history or an appraisal.

Make the history easy to review

Keep photographs and documents with the firearm they describe. Include the provenance material in your collection PDF export so the history can be reviewed together with maintenance and range records. Review personal information before sharing a catalog.
